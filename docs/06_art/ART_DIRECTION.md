# GOOD HUMAN! — Art Direction v0.2

## Current target

GOOD HUMAN! is a modern urban 3D game with grounded cinematic realism viewed from a dog's scale.

The owner decision on 2026-09-19 makes the supplied P-03 Street Brawl storyboard the in-game visual north star—not only a composition reference. Runtime characters, environments, lighting, materials, animation and effects should converge toward its photographic credibility and emotional tone within mobile performance constraints.

Primary references:

- `docs/06_art/dog_agency/P03_STREET_BRAWL_STORYBOARD.png`
- `assets/_source/design/dog_agency/p03_combat_mood_target_01.png`
- `assets/_source/design/dog_agency/p03_tension_confrontation_storyboard_01.png`

Earlier 2D hand-drawn/cartoon and low-poly directions are superseded for final visible gameplay. Existing low-poly assets remain valid functional prototypes until replaced; they are not final-art candidates merely because they work in runtime.

## Visual thesis

> Explore as the dog. Fight through the dog's eyes.

- Contemporary streets and parks feel lived-in, familiar and materially believable.
- Humans feel large because the camera sits at dog height, not because anatomy is distorted.
- Warm natural light and ordinary clothing keep the world humane beneath its absurd premise.
- Combat feels physical through anticipation, contact, balance, recoil, cloth and sound.
- Dog–human attachment is visible through gaze, leash behavior, touch and proximity.

Avoid:

- generic fantasy or glossy mobile-RPG presentation;
- low-detail asset-pack appearance for visible final characters;
- toy-like low-poly surfaces as a final style;
- heroic martial-arts uniforms or superhero anatomy;
- heavy outlines, flat toon ramps or mixed pixel/vector/painted presentation;
- excessive bloom, gore, damage numbers, giant hit flashes or combat arenas;
- photographic background plates behind visibly synthetic characters.

## Fidelity hierarchy

Spend visual budget in this order:

1. player dog;
2. human owner;
3. dog/human motion, gaze, hands, faces and leash contact;
4. opponent pair during encounters;
5. immediate ground plane and dog-eye foreground;
6. lighting, atmosphere and nearby world reactions;
7. neighborhood structures and midground props;
8. distant crowds and background dressing.

Photorealistic credibility does not require uniform polygon density. Strong silhouette, physically believable motion, coherent materials and lighting take priority over hidden geometry.

## Characters

Dog and owner are the visual identity of GOOD HUMAN! and require S-level final quality.

- Natural proportions, grounded weight and believable joint motion.
- Faces and eyes must support recognition, concern, relief and affection at mobile size.
- Dog fur uses readable clumps and direction rather than uniformly expensive strands.
- Hands, paws, collars, harnesses and leash anchors receive close-camera quality.
- Ordinary contemporary clothing uses believable fabric response, seams, wear and layering.

The human system still supports modular variation:

Body + Head/Face + Hair + Top + Bottom + Shoes + Accessory.

Appearance must not reveal true combat strength. A cardigan, hoodie, work jacket or tote bag cannot become shorthand for difficulty.

## Environment

The neighborhood is contemporary, warm and slightly imperfect:

- asphalt and paving show restrained wear, dirt and repaired areas;
- trees, benches, bins, fences and shopfronts use plausible scale and material response;
- golden-hour warmth is a signature target, not a permanent orange filter;
- overcast and evening conditions must retain the same material realism and dog-height composition;
- background pedestrians and animals react economically to conflict.

Free CC0 environment bases may be adapted, but scale, material values, texel density, lighting and signage must be unified. Untouched source assets never become final simply through import.

## Lighting and color

- Physically plausible key, fill and contact shadow relationships.
- Warm sunlight against natural park greens and muted urban neutrals.
- Teal remains a restrained player relationship accent on harness/leash.
- Amber supports attention and warmth; coral supports brief impact presentation.
- Color cannot replace pose, material or motion readability.
- Depth of field is selective and mild enough to keep fighters, feet and leashes readable.

## Camera and composition

- Exploration: dog-height third-person with the dog visible.
- Tension: camera lowers and advances without a cut.
- Combat candidate: dog-eye first-person with automatic CombatCenter framing.
- Resolution: hold the emotional beat, then return smoothly to third-person.

Do not use fisheye distortion, violent auto-rotation, cinematic letterboxing or framing that removes dog agency. Human feet and leash ownership remain legible whenever the encounter depends on balance or pull.

## Animation and impact

- Anticipation, planted feet, torso rotation, contact compression and recovery create weight.
- Light and heavy attacks differ in timing, commitment, recoil and knockback.
- Hitstop and camera impulse reinforce physical contact rather than hide weak poses.
- Condition reads through spine, feet, recovery rhythm, head and breath before UI.
- Victory and defeat end through owner–dog behavior, not only result text.

## UI

UI stays dog-perspective and restrained:

- dog tags, leash motifs, paw/footprint and scent cues;
- minimal contextual Bark and Interact controls;
- no conventional RPG combat hotbar;
- UI never competes with faces, hands, leash anchors or contact poses.

The graphic layer may be more stylized than the world, but its material, color and motion must sit comfortably over cinematic 3D rather than resemble a separate cartoon game.

## Production rule

Every new visible P-03 asset must answer: does it move the runtime closer to the storyboard? If not, it remains a prototype, reference or rejected direction. Performance simplification is acceptable; a return to toy-like low-poly final art is not.
