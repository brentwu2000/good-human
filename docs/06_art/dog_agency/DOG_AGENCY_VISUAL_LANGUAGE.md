# GOOD HUMAN! — Dog Agency Visual Language v0.1

Status: REVIEW  
Reference: `assets/_source/design/dog_agency/dog_agency_action_study_01.png`

## Chosen direction

Use a warm, readable low-poly dog silhouette with teal harness/leash accents and amber attention marks. The dog remains the foreground author of the moment; humans and opponent dogs react in secondary layers.

The study is a composition and behavior reference, not a final render. It establishes how a dog-height camera should communicate agency without pausing the seamless fight.

## Bark

- Dog body compresses slightly, head lifts, mouth opens, ears point forward.
- A small amber double-chevron or three-ray mark appears near the muzzle for 0.35–0.6 seconds.
- Opponent dog rotates toward the player dog and may answer; the human reaction stays in world space.
- The bark mark is directional, never a large skill-button effect or screen-wide alert.

## Leash tension

| State | Leash | Owner body | Screen read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slack | curved, low sag | normal stride | dog is exploring |
| Light | shallow curve | one shoulder follows | dog is leading |
| Strong | near-straight | torso leans, feet widen | owner is being pulled |
| Dangerous | straight with visible pull vector | stumble/recover pose | stop or reposition |

The teal leash remains the primary relationship cue. Do not use a numeric meter in the player HUD.

## Combat agency composition

Keep the player dog in the near foreground or near flank, with the owner leash visible. Human-versus-human combat remains readable in the middle distance. Opponent dog reactions use silhouette and head direction before text.

## Camera notes

This direction is authored for the accepted dog-height chase camera. Keep the dog’s back, head and harness within the lower-middle safe area. Pull back only when combat framing requires both humans; preserve the leash in frame whenever possible.
