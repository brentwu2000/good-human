# P03-D01 — Combat Mood Target v0.1

Status: REVIEW

Target image: `assets/_source/design/dog_agency/p03_combat_mood_target_01.png`

## Selected direction

Match the supplied P-03 storyboard as closely as practical in cinematography, material realism, lighting and emotional weight. Exploration remains warm and grounded; combat feels immediate because ordinary humans become large from dog-eye height.

The target image is generated concept art rather than a final runtime capture, but its grounded cinematic realism is the required direction for visible P-03 gameplay. Runtime assets may simplify hidden geometry and distant detail for performance; foreground characters, materials, lighting, camera scale and emotional behavior must visibly converge toward it.

## Visual pillars

- Camera sits approximately 35–45 cm above the ground.
- Player dog's ears/muzzle and teal leash establish authorship without blocking the fight.
- Both human bodies remain physically readable from planted feet through the contact point.
- Warm late-afternoon backlight creates beauty around an uncomfortable, ordinary confrontation.
- Impact comes from anatomy, compression, recoil, cloth, dust and restrained motion blur—not fantasy flashes.
- Rival dog and black leash keep the opposing relationship visible at the frame edge.
- Background witnesses react naturally enough that the world acknowledges escalation.

## Style lock

P-03 targets grounded cinematic realism:

- believable skin, cotton, denim, shoe rubber, pavement and leash webbing;
- natural body proportions and ordinary contemporary clothes;
- shallow depth of field used selectively, never hiding the two fighters or either leash;
- amber sunlight, natural greens, muted earth tones and a restrained teal player cue;
- intimate documentary tension rather than heroic fight-poster posing.

Avoid low-poly final presentation, glossy mobile-RPG characters, martial-arts uniforms, superhero anatomy, gore, weapons, combat arenas, damage numbers and oversized impact effects.

## Composition read order

1. punch contact and receiver recoil;
2. player's owner as the emotionally relevant human;
3. foreground dog POV and teal leash;
4. opponent human–dog pair and their dark leash;
5. park witnesses and golden-hour environment.

## Production translation

Runtime art should translate the target rather than reproduce every photographic detail:

- prioritize human silhouette, footwork, contact poses and leash continuity;
- spend material detail on faces, hands, jackets, denim, dog fur and the immediate ground plane;
- use lighting and grading to unify simpler background assets;
- keep distant witnesses lower-detail and animation-efficient;
- preserve the same low camera scale even when performance requires simpler geometry or shaders.

## Acceptance

- Without UI or combat text, a viewer identifies two fighting humans, the player dog, the rival dog and which human each leash belongs to.
- Humans feel imposing because of dog-eye scale, not distorted anatomy.
- The contact reads as a landed heavy punch rather than two static characters touching.
- The image feels related to the supplied storyboard in lighting, realism, lens language and emotional tone.
- The frame remains about being the dog, not watching a generic human fighting game.

## Generation record

Built-in OpenAI image generation was used with the owner-supplied P-03 storyboard as a style and cinematography reference. One corrective edit removed an unintended extra handler and connected the rival dog's leash to the rival human. No third-party character or environment asset was imported.
